# The crash-as-abstain rule and what it actually measures

Working notes, not a paper artifact. Nothing here is regenerated by a table or
figure builder and nothing here is cited by one. Read-only with respect to
`results/`: every input is a frozen artifact in `results/` or a regrade
artifact in the scratchpad, and nothing under `results/` was written.

This file exists because the rule below has grown from an accounting
convention into a **metrics-section claim in its own right**. It was previously
buried in a file about judge confidence, which is the wrong home for it.

## 1. The rule

From the governing definitions:

> a judge verdict of `"error"` (crash/unparseable) **counts as an abstain**,
> not as an excluded trial

The rule is correct and should stay. Dropping crash verdicts from the recall
denominator was a real bug: it inflated Figure 3's gpt-oss bars to
1.00/1.00/0.38 instead of the correct 0.93/1.00/0.36, and
`test_error_injection.py` carries a regression test for it
(`metrics: crash verdict counts as abstain`).

**Nothing here argues for changing the rule.** What follows is about what the
resulting numbers mean.

## 2. Three things the rule collapses into one bucket

The code produces non-committed verdicts by three distinct routes:

| route | how it appears in stored data | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| deliberate | `verdict: "abstain"` with real prose reasoning | the judge read the step and declined to rule |
| unparseable | `verdict: "unparseable"`, or `"abstain"` with reasoning `"(could not parse judge response as JSON)"` | the judge answered, the answer was not machine-readable |
| crash / API | `verdict: "error"` with an exception string in `reasoning` | no answer was obtained at all |

`src/llm_judge.py::_parse_verdict` has two unparseable paths: no JSON object in
the reply, and a JSON object whose `verdict` field is outside
`{"passed","failed","abstain"}`. `LLMJudgeGrader.grade_step` then maps
`unparseable -> abstain`, so in the **injection** reports the unparseable case
is indistinguishable from a deliberate abstain by verdict alone; only the
`reasoning` sentinel separates them. In the **human-set** files
(`judge_step_verdicts*.json`) the raw `judge_step` output is stored, so
`verdict: "unparseable"` survives literally, along with `judge_raw`.

Classifying by verdict alone will therefore undercount unparseables on the
human set. That is a live trap: it produced a wrong first count during this
audit.

## 4. The headline finding

**Every one of gpt-oss's injection abstains is an HTTP 429 rate-limit error.**
Not one is a judgment. This holds in the frozen data (7/7) and in the
repaired-gold1 regrade (6/6, all carried over from frozen; the 18 fresh gold1
calls had zero transport errors).

So the published gpt-oss injection abstain rate — 0.059 frozen, 0.051 on the
repair — **measures the provider's rate limiter, not the model.**

And the asymmetry is systematic:

> **gpt-oss-120b deliberately abstains once in 309 trials** (191 human + 118
> injection). **claude-sonnet-4-5 does so 13 times.**

These are two very different behaviours. The rule counts them identically.

## 5. Blast radius: which reported cells depend on an artifact abstain

An artifact abstain does not sit inertly in an "abstain" column. It enters
denominators:

- **strict recall** = `TP / (TP + FN + AbsE)` — an artifact abstain on an
  *error* trial inflates the denominator and **lowers** recall.
- **covered FPR** = `FP / (FP + TN)` — an artifact abstain on a *negative*
  trial is excluded, **shrinking** the denominator and **raising** FPR.
- **coverage** and **abstain rate** — directly.

### gpt-oss, injection row: the whole abstain column WAS artifact — now resolved

| cell | before | exposure | after re-collection |
|---|---|---|---|
| abstain | 0.059 | 100% rate-limit | **0.000** |
| coverage | 0.941 | `1 - abst` | **1.000** |
| strict recall | 0.851 | all 6 `AbsE` were 429s | **0.894** |
| covered FPR | 0.087 | excluded negative was a 429 | **0.000** |

The recall figure was the sharpest case: **0.851 was a floor imposed by rate
limiting, not a measurement of the model's recall.** The re-collection settled
it at **0.894**. Four of the five recovered error trials were caught; one,
`gold6 s2 rename_variable`, was a genuine miss (`passed`, 0.97) — so recovering
the trials converted one artifact abstain into a real false negative rather than
into a catch. The single recovered negative, `gold6 s2 distribute`, came back
`passed` (0.99), a true negative, leaving covered FPR at 0.000.

### claude, human row

4 of 9 abstains are unparseable. One of them, `Q09.4`, is on an error step, and
claude's human `AbsE` is exactly 1 — so **its human strict recall
0.769 = 10/13 has an artifact sitting in the denominator.** The other three are
non-error and shrink the covered-FPR denominator behind 0.100.

### claude, injection row

The `0.286` covered FPR on the repaired fixture depends on
`gold1 s2 commute_operands` moving `passed -> abstain`, and that abstain is an
unparseable reply. See `confidence_equivalents_notes.md` §8a.

### Cascade (Table 3)

Every symbolic abstain routes to the judge (`used_judge` is 72–73 of 118), so
the cascade rows inherit the judge-side artifact abstains wholesale. No cascade
cell is insulated.

### Clean rows

The gpt-oss **human** row is the only judge row with no artifact abstain at all:
its single non-committed verdict is deliberate.

## 6. What to say in the metrics section

Defensible:

- The rule is correct and stays; dropping crashes was the Figure 3 bug.
- Report the breakdown, not just the abstain rate. An abstain rate is not
  interpretable as judge behaviour without it.
- State plainly that gpt-oss's injection abstains are rate-limit failures, so
  its abstain rate and coverage on that testbed are not model properties.
- The 1-vs-13 deliberate-abstain contrast is a real, reportable difference in
  judge behaviour, and it is *invisible* in the published abstain rates.

Not defensible without more work:

- Comparing the two judges' abstain rates as though they measure the same
  thing.
- Quoting gpt-oss's injection strict recall as 0.851. That figure is superseded;
  the measured value after re-collection is **0.894**.
- Any claim that gpt-oss "abstains less" as a model property. On the human set,
  where there were no transport failures, it abstained once and claude five
  times deliberately — a real difference, but a much smaller one than the
  injection rows imply.

`results/` was updated in the same pass as the gold1 promotion, and the

re-collected trials, the date, and a `two_vintage_warning`.

**What remains live in this file:** the claude injection exposure (its 0.286
covered FPR still depends on an unparseable reply), the claude human-set
exposure (4 of 9 abstains unparseable, one of them on an error step), the
1-vs-13 deliberate-abstain asymmetry, and the general point that the rule
collapses three distinct events into one bucket.

**What is no longer live:** every claim that gpt-oss's *injection* abstain rate,
coverage, or recall is rate-limiter artifact. That row is clean now.

One caveat the fix introduces: the gpt-oss injection row is now **two-vintage**
(see root `CLAUDE.md`). Trading an artifact abstain for a cross-session
comparison is the right trade here, but it is a trade, not a free repair.
