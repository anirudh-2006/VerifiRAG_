## 2. What a pair is

The injection generator emits, per mutable step site, one trial per mutation
operator. Two of the 14 operator families matter here:

- **`equivalent`** (severity `equivalent`): `commute_operands`, `expand_power`,
  `distribute`. The mutated expression is algebraically equal to the gold one.
- **`control`** (severity `control`): `no_op`, identity. Verified: for all 14
  control trials, `mutated_expr == original_expr` byte for byte.

A **pair** is one `equivalent` trial joined to the `control` trial at the same
`(gold_id, step_index)`. The control is the same derivation, the same step, the
same prompt scaffolding, and a string the judge has already seen unmodified.
Both members are ground-truth negatives: the correct verdict is PASS for both.
Any verdict difference inside a pair is attributable to surface form alone.

10 `equivalent` trials survived generation (32 were skipped as inapplicable),
spanning 8 distinct step sites, so there are 10 pairs. The same 10 pairs exist
for both judges; the two judges saw identical inputs.

Symbolic is a control on the controls: across all 24 negatives (10 equivalent +
14 identity) it commits **zero FAIL verdicts**, passing 8/10 equivalent and
12/14 identity, abstaining on the two gold1 steps. The rewrite sensitivity below
is a judge property, not a property of the trials.

### 3.1 gold1 step1, `commute_operands` — FAIL(0.95) -> PASS(0.95)  **[FLIP]**

- concept: Time-shift property
- original: `exp(-I*omega*t0)*integrate(x*exp(-I*omega*t), (t, -oo, oo))`
- mutated: `integrate(x*exp(-I*omega*t), (t, -oo, oo)) * exp(-I*omega*t0)`
- rewrite rationale (FAIL): "The step attempts to factor out exp(-I*omega*t0)
  from the integral, but this is incorrect. The correct factorization is
  exp(-I*omega*t) * exp(I*omega*t0) = exp(-I*omega*(t-t0)), so exp(I*omega*t0)
  should be factored out, not exp(-I*omega*t0). The sign in the exponent is
  wrong."
- control rationale (PASS): "The factor exp(-I*omega*t0) is independent of the
  integration variable t, so it can be factored out of the integral. Expanding
  exp(-I*omega*(t - t0)) = exp(-I*omega*t)*exp(I*omega*t0) and factoring gives
  the stated result."
- symbolic: abstain on both.

The two rationales assert the same expansion and reach opposite verdicts. The
control rationale derives `exp(+I*omega*t0)` and then calls `exp(-I*omega*t0)`
"the stated result", which is internally inconsistent. See §8: this site is also
where the gold fixture itself is in dispute.

### 3.2 gold1 step2, `commute_operands` — PASS(0.95) -> PASS(0.95)

- original: `exp(-I*omega*t0)*X`; mutated: `X * exp(-I*omega*t0)`
- both accepted as a definitional substitution of the inner integral by `X`.
- symbolic: abstain on both.

### 3.3 gold2 step1, `commute_operands` — ABSTAIN(0.0) -> PASS(1.0)  *[not a flip]*

- concept: Pure algebra, quadratic identity
- original: `omega**2 + 2*omega + 1`; mutated: `2*omega + 1 + omega**2`
- rewrite rationale: "(could not parse judge response as JSON)". Counted as an
  abstain per the governing rule, and it is one of claude's two `abstain_eq` on
  injection. Not scored as a false positive, but it is still a non-identical
  outcome on a byte-identical-modulo-reordering negative.
- symbolic: passed both.

### 3.4 gold2 step1, `expand_power` — PASS(1.0) -> PASS(1.0)

- mutated: `(omega)*(omega) + 2*omega + 1`. Accepted, rationale expands the
  binomial and matches term by term. symbolic: passed both.

### 3.5 gold3 step1, `commute_operands` — FAIL(0.95) -> PASS(1.0)  **[FLIP]**

- concept: Trig identity
- original: `2*sin(omega)*cos(omega)`; mutated: `sin(omega)*cos(omega) * 2`
- rewrite rationale (FAIL), **quoted in full**: "The double angle identity is
  sin(2ω) = 2sin(ω)cos(ω), not sin(ω)cos(ω)·2. While these are algebraically
  equal by commutativity, the step claims to apply an identity but produces the
  factors in non-standard order, suggesting a potential misunderstanding of the
  identity itself."
- control rationale (PASS): "This is the correct application of the double angle
  identity for sine: sin(2θ) = 2sin(θ)cos(θ). The substitution of omega for the
  angle variable is valid."
- symbolic: passed both.

This is the cleanest instance in the study. The judge states the equality holds
and returns FAIL anyway, on stylistic grounds ("non-standard order").

### 3.6 gold4 step1, `commute_operands` — FAIL(0.95) -> PASS(1.0)  **[FLIP]**

- concept: Euler identity in sinc
- original: `2*I*sin(omega)/(I*omega)`; mutated: `I*sin(omega)/(I*omega) * 2`
- rewrite rationale (FAIL): "Using Euler's formula, exp(I*omega) - exp(-I*omega)
  = 2*I*sin(omega), so the previous expression becomes 2*I*sin(omega)/(I*omega)
  = 2*sin(omega)/omega. The current step has an extra factor of I in the
  numerator that should have canceled."
- control rationale (PASS): "Using Euler's formula, exp(I*omega) -
  exp(-I*omega) = 2*I*sin(omega). Substituting this into the numerator of the
  previous step directly yields 2*I*sin(omega)/(I*omega)."
- symbolic: passed both.

Here the reordering induces a substantive misreading: the judge reads
`I*sin(omega)/(I*omega) * 2` as a step that failed to cancel `I`, even though
the control form carries the identical uncancelled `I` and is accepted. The
misreading is caused by the trailing `* 2` moving the leading coefficient away
from the front.

### 3.7 gold4 step2, `commute_operands` — PASS(0.95) -> PASS(1.0)

- original: `2*sin(omega)/omega`; mutated: `sin(omega)/omega * 2`. Accepted, and
  the rationale explicitly invokes commutativity. Same operator and same gold as
  §3.6, opposite outcome. symbolic: passed both.

### 3.8 gold6 step2, `distribute` — PASS(1.0) -> PASS(1.0)

- concept: Partial fraction expansion
- original: `1/(2*(s - 1)) - 1/(2*(s + 1))`; mutated: `1/(2*s - 2*1) - 1/(2*(s + 1))`
- accepted: "2*s - 2*1 = 2(s-1) is just an unfactored form of the first
  denominator." symbolic: passed both.

### 3.9 gold8 step1, `commute_operands` — PASS(1.0) -> PASS(1.0)

- concept: Derivative of polynomial
- original: `3*x**2`; mutated: `x**2 * 3`. Accepted with explicit appeal to
  algebraic equivalence. symbolic: passed both.

### 3.10 gold8 step1, `expand_power` — FAIL(0.95) -> PASS(1.0)  **[FLIP]**

- original: `3*x**2`; mutated: `3*(x)*(x)`
- rewrite rationale (FAIL), **quoted in full**: "The derivative of x³ with
  respect to x is 3x², not 3·x·x. While 3·x·x could be interpreted as 3x², the
  step claims this is the result of differentiation applied to the previous
  step, but the previous step already was the differentiation operation
  diff(x**3, x), so this step appears to be re-stating the result incorrectly or
  redundantly."
- control rationale (PASS): "The derivative of x³ with respect to x is correctly
  computed using the power rule: d/dx(x^n) = n*x^(n-1), giving 3*x²."
- symbolic: passed both.

Note the same site under `commute_operands` (§3.9) is accepted, so the objection
is not to the step's position in the derivation, which is unchanged, but to the
surface form `3*(x)*(x)`.

## 6. Finding F2 must be scoped per judge

The four points this file exists to record:

### F2a. Claude flips on 3 of 4, and concedes the algebra in two of them

**Revised 2026-08-24.** On the repaired fixture, **three** of claude's four
FAIL verdicts on equivalence-preserving rewrites have a PASS on the
byte-identical identity control at the same step (gold3 s1, gold4 s1, gold8 s1).
The fourth, gold1 s1, is no longer a flip: claude now FAILs the control too, so
both halves agree. The flip rate against controls is **3/4**.

The rewrite family no longer accounts for 100% of claude's injection false
positives either: 4 of its 6 FPs are rewrites, and 2 are identity controls.

Note *why* gold1 s1 stopped being a flip. Not because claude was vindicated —
the repaired step is machine-verified correct and claude's proposed correction
is wrong by a factor of `exp(a*t0)`. It stopped being a flip because claude
fails the control as well, moving that trial out of the flip count and into the
FPR. See §8a.

Two of the four rationales state the algebraic equality and return FAIL anyway:

> "The double angle identity is sin(2ω) = 2sin(ω)cos(ω), not sin(ω)cos(ω)·2.
> **While these are algebraically equal by commutativity**, the step claims to
> apply an identity but produces the factors in non-standard order, suggesting a
> potential misunderstanding of the identity itself." (gold3 step1)

> "The derivative of x³ with respect to x is 3x², not 3·x·x. **While 3·x·x could
> be interpreted as 3x²**, the step claims this is the result of differentiation
> applied to the previous step, but the previous step already was the
> differentiation operation diff(x**3, x), so this step appears to be re-stating
> the result incorrectly or redundantly." (gold8 step1)

These are not equivalence-detection failures. The judge detects the equivalence
and then grades presentation. That distinction matters for how the finding is
described: the failure mode is a grading-criterion failure, not a mathematical
one.

### F2b. gpt-oss has zero flips, and its two FPs were one gold dispute — now confirmed and gone

gpt-oss shows 0 PASS -> FAIL flips across all 10 pairs. Its pre-repair
per-family false positive rates of **1/9** (equivalent) and **1/14** (control)
were the *same step*, gold1 step1, appearing once under each family: the rewrite
trial and its identity control both received FAIL with the same 0.96 confidence
and substantively the same rationale.

**Confirmed 2026-08-24.** The hypothesis was right. With gold1 repaired, gpt-oss
PASSes both the rewrite (0.97) and the control (0.97), and its injection false
positives drop to **zero**: equivalent **0/10**, control **0/14**, overall
covered FPR **0.000**, precision **1.000**. Its two apparent F2 instances were
entirely an artifact of the defective fixture. Because the control is byte-identical to the
gold expression, that verdict cannot be a response to the rewrite. It is a
dispute with the gold fixture (§8), and it is counted twice only because the
trial grid pairs every site with a `no_op`.

Consequence for the paper: **gpt-oss-120b contributes zero instances of F2.**
F2 must be stated as a property of claude-sonnet-4-5 on this suite, not as a
general property of LLM judges. A sentence of the form "LLM judges fail
equivalence-preserving rewrites" is not supported by this data; one judge of two
does, and the other's apparent instances dissolve on inspection.

### F2c. No confidence threshold separates the rewrite FPs from true catches

Sweep over "commit FAIL only if confidence >= tau", injection suite.

claude-sonnet-4-5 (TP = 85, FP = 4, all 4 FPs at confidence 0.95):

| tau | TP retained | catches lost | FP | equivalent-family FP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.85 | 85 | 0 (0.0%) | 4 | 4 |
| 0.95 | 79 | 6 (7.1%) | 4 | 4 |
| 1.00 | 10 | **75 (88.2%)** | 0 | 0 |
| >1.00 | 0 | 85 (100%) | 0 | 0 |

The rewrite false positives sit at 0.95, and so do 69 of the 85 true catches.
The first threshold that removes all four costs 88.2% of the true catches. There
is no intermediate setting: claude emits only three distinct confidence values
for FAIL verdicts on injection (0.85 × 6, 0.95 × 73, 1.00 × 10), so the sweep
has three reachable operating points and the rewrite FPs are inseparable from
the bulk of the TP mass by construction.

gpt-oss-120b (TP = 80, FP = 2, both at confidence 0.96):

| tau | TP retained | catches lost | FP |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.85 | 80 | 0 (0.0%) | 2 |
| 0.92 | 79 | 1 (1.2%) | 2 |
| 0.95 | 77 | 3 (3.8%) | 2 |
| 0.96 | 71 | 9 (11.2%) | 2 |
| 0.97 | 54 | **26 (32.5%)** | 0 |
| 0.99 | 37 | 43 (53.8%) | 0 |

The 32.5% figure is the cost of clearing gpt-oss's FPs at tau = 0.97, and it is
**irrelevant to F2**: neither of the two suppressed FPs is a rewrite failure
(§F2b), so the threshold is not buying equivalence robustness. It is buying
agreement with a gold fixture that is itself under dispute.

Human set, for completeness. claude emits only **two** distinct FAIL

4 at 0.95, alongside 6 of its 10 true catches at 0.85. Raising tau to 0.95 cuts
FP 17 -> 4 but loses 60% of catches (10 -> 4). gpt-oss on the human set: FAIL
confidences 0.90/0.92/0.95/0.96; tau = 0.96 clears all 3 FPs at the cost of 2 of
6 catches (33.3%).

for claude the confidence signal is close to categorical, two or three levels
depending on testbed, so a "threshold" is a choice among two or three points,
not a continuous operating curve. It should not be drawn or described as one.

## 8. OPEN: the gold1 step1 sign dispute is unadjudicated

> **Adjudicated 2026-08-24 (marked 2026-08-25).** No longer open. The dispute
> was resolved in the judges' favour on the reading question and against the
> fixture: gold1 was found defective on independent grounds
> (`fixture_integrity_notes.md` §1) and re-authored as a concrete derivation.
> Both judges' objections to the old s1 were correct. Item 1's prediction for
> gpt-oss was confirmed exactly; item 2's prediction for claude was **wrong**,
> in the direction described in §8a. Kept as written for the record.

Both judges object to gold1 step1 on the same grounds, and the objection is not
obviously wrong.

The fixture (`src/error_injection.py`, "Time-shift property"):

```
step 1: integrate(x*exp(-I*omega*(t - t0)), (t, -oo, oo))   # "Start with the FT of the shifted signal"
step 2: exp(-I*omega*t0)*integrate(x*exp(-I*omega*t), (t, -oo, oo))   # "Split the exponential"
step 3: exp(-I*omega*t0)*X
```

The trial's `step_index = 1` is step 2 above, the "split the exponential" step.

As mathematics about the shift property, `FT{x(t - t0)} = exp(-I*omega*t0)·X(omega)`,
the gold is the textbook result. As a manipulation of the literal expression
written in step 1, it is not: `x` is a bare SymPy symbol, not a function of `t`,
so step 1 says `x · exp(-I*omega*t) · exp(+I*omega*t0)` integrated over `t`, and
pulling the constant out gives `exp(+I*omega*t0)·∫x·exp(-I*omega*t)dt`. Both
judges perform exactly that expansion and report the sign as wrong. gpt-oss:
"exp(-I*omega*(t - t0)) = exp(-I*omega*t) * exp(I*omega*t0) … The sign of the
factor outside the integral is incorrect." claude, on the rewrite: "…so
exp(I*omega*t0) should be factored out, not exp(-I*omega*t0)."

The fixture encodes the time shift in the exponential kernel rather than in the
signal argument. Under the intended reading, step 1 stands for
`∫x(t-t0)exp(-I*omega*t)dt` and the gold is right. Under the written reading, the
gold step is a sign error.

**This is not resolved here, and it should not be resolved by editing anything.**
Recording the consequences:

1. If the written reading is correct, gold1 step1 is a **gold error**, and the
   two gpt-oss verdicts currently scored as false positives (Table 1 injection,
   gpt-oss FP = 2) are correct detections against a bad fixture. Both of gpt-oss's
   injection false positives are that one step. Its covered FPR would go from
   0.087 to 0.000, and the negatives-family rates 1/9 and 1/14 would go to 0/9
   and 0/14. **This is a prediction and it has not been checked. The gpt-oss
   half of the repaired-fixture regrade has not run** (§8a). The claude half
   contradicted the analogous prediction for claude, so do not treat this one as
   settled either.
2. One of claude's four flips (pair 1) is at the same site, and pair 1 stops
   being a flip under the repair. **It does not become a correct detection.**
   See §8a: on the repaired fixture claude fails the control as well as the
   rewrite, so the pair fails the both-committed-and-differing test for a flip

   FAILs on rewrites, not 3 of 3, and claude gains false positives rather than

#### RESOLVED: the 0.286 reconciles once the third moved trial is counted

Originally recorded as open; the reasoning below is kept verbatim as written,
with the resolution appended at the end of the block.

From the frozen cells, claude's injection negatives are 24 = 14 `no_op`
controls + 10 equivalent rewrites, of which 22 are committed (13 controls and
9 rewrites; one abstain in each family, §5). Baseline covered FPR is
4/(4+18) = 0.182. Adding two controls as false positives gives FP = 6,
TN = 16, still 22 committed:

```
6/22 = 0.273        not 0.286
6/21 = 0.2857       0.286
```

So 0.286 implies **21** committed negatives, one fewer than baseline. Some
negative-side commitment other than the two controls must also have changed in
the regrade, most likely a trial moving into or out of abstain, or a change in
the mutable-step-site count for the re-authored fixture. The regrade artifact
should say which; this file does not guess. **Do not carry 0.286 into either
paper until that one trial is identified**, and do not silently substitute
0.273 for it either.

**Resolution.** The trial is **`gold1 s2 commute_operands`**, which moved
`passed -> abstain`. Three negative-side trials moved, not two:

| trial | severity | old | new |
|---|---|---|---|
| gold1 s1 `no_op` | control | passed | failed |
| gold1 s2 `no_op` | control | passed | failed |
| **gold1 s2 `commute_operands`** | equivalent | **passed** | **abstain** |

The two controls do what this section predicted (FP 4 -> 6, TN 18 -> 16). The
third removes a committed negative from the denominator altogether, so TN is

```
OLD  FP=4 TN=18 abstain=2  committed=22   4/22 = 0.1818
NEW  FP=6 TN=15 abstain=3  committed=21   6/21 = 0.2857
```

Negative trials total 24 in both runs, so the alternative hypothesis above — a
