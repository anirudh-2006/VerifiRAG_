## 1. Policies

Each judge's verdict is reduced to one of {failed, passed, abstain}, where
abstain absorbs a crash or unparseable reply, exactly as in Table 1.

| policy | rule |
|---|---|
| UNION | flag if either judge commits FAIL. Lone FAIL + abstain = flag. Abstains only when both judges abstain. |
| INTERSECTION | flag only if both commit FAIL. Lone FAIL + abstain = committed PASS. Abstains only when both judges abstain. |
| AGREEMENT | commit only where both judges commit and agree. Any abstain from either judge, or any both-committed disagreement, is an abstain. |

## 5. Findings

### F1. INTERSECTION and AGREEMENT are the same operating point under strict recall

They are not two points on a curve. On both testbeds they have identical TP and
identical FP:

| | TP | FP | strict recall | FN | AbsE | FN+AbsE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| INTERSECTION, human | 4 | 2 | 0.308 | 9 | 0 | 9 |
| AGREEMENT, human | 4 | 2 | 0.308 | 1 | 8 | 9 |
| INTERSECTION, injection | 84 | 0 | 0.894 | 10 | 0 | 10 |
| AGREEMENT, injection | 84 | 0 | 0.894 | 2 | 8 | 10 |

Both flag an error only when both judges commit FAIL, so they select exactly the
same items as positives, and strict recall charges an abstain-on-error the same
as a miss. `FN + AbsE` is therefore invariant (9 and 10), and recall is equal by
construction, not by coincidence.

What differs is only how the non-flagged errors are *labeled*. INTERSECTION
commits them as passes (FN); AGREEMENT abstains on them (AbsE). Two things move
as a consequence:

- **Coverage.** 1.000 -> 0.832 human, 1.000 -> 0.856 injection.
- **The FPR denominator.** Same FP, fewer committed negatives: human TN 176 ->
  152, so FPR 0.011 -> 0.013; injection TN 24 -> 15, so FPR 0.000 -> 0.000 (gpt-oss now commits no injection FPs at all).
  AGREEMENT's *worse* FPR is not more false positives, it is the same single or
  pair of false positives divided by a smaller pool.

Presenting these as two distinct operating points would be wrong. They are one
detection rule under two abstain conventions. If a comparison needs them
separated, the honest axis is coverage, not recall or FPR.

### F2. AGREEMENT's abstains concentrate on errors only on the human set, and reverse on injection

> human-set block below is unaffected. Recomputed 2026-08-25, the injection
> block is:
>
> ```
> error_injection:
>   error items       8/94   = 0.085   (one abstained 2, both 0, disagreed 6)
>   non-error items   9/24   = 0.375   (one abstained 3, both 0, disagreed 6)
>   ratio 0.23x
> ```
>
> **The finding is unchanged and is in fact strengthened**: the direction still
> reverses relative to the human set, and more sharply. The stale "both 2" in
> the block below is now impossible, since gpt-oss abstains on no injection
> trial. The caveat still governs: 24 non-error trials is a small denominator,
> so quote the direction, not 0.23.

```
human_labels:
  error items       8/13   = 0.615   (one abstained 2, both 0, disagreed 6)
  non-error items  24/178  = 0.135   (one abstained 8, both 0, disagreed 16)
  ratio 4.56x -- AGREEMENT declines to grade where the errors are

error_injection:
  error items      17/94   = 0.181   (one abstained 9, both 2, disagreed 6)
  non-error items   7/24   = 0.292   (one abstained 3, both 0, disagreed 4)
  ratio 0.62x -- AGREEMENT abstains LESS often on errors here than on non-errors
```

On the human set the reading is damaging: an abstention policy meant to route
hard cases to a human is 4.56x more likely to abstain precisely on the steps
that contain errors, so most of what it hands back is the part that mattered.
Six of the eight are both-committed disagreements, meaning the two judges
actively contradicted each other on 6 of 13 errors.

**On injection the effect goes the other way** (0.62x): AGREEMENT abstains on
18.1% of error trials and 29.2% of non-error trials. Any claim that
agreement-based abstention "concentrates on errors" must be scoped to the human
set explicitly. Stated unscoped it is contradicted by the other testbed in this
same study.

The two testbeds differ in a way that plausibly explains the flip, and it is
worth naming rather than presenting the reversal as a mystery: injection errors
are synthetic single-operator mutations of known-correct gold steps, which both
judges tend to catch and agree on (76 of 94 caught by both), whereas the 24
non-error injection trials include the equivalence-preserving rewrites the
judges disagree about. The human-set errors are naturally occurring and much
less legible. But note that 13 and 24 are small denominators on the two sides
that drive each ratio, so neither number should be quoted with more precision
than "the direction reverses".

### F3. Neither ensemble Pareto-dominates a single judge, on either testbed

Against the better single judge on each axis:

| testbed | ensemble | vs | recall | FPR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| human | UNION 0.923 / 0.101 | claude 0.769 / 0.100 | better | worse |
| human | INTERSECTION 0.308 / 0.011 | gpt-oss 0.462 / 0.017 | worse | better |
| injection | UNION 0.957 / 0.250 | claude 0.957 / 0.286 | equal | better |
| injection | INTERSECTION 0.894 / 0.000 | gpt-oss 0.894 / 0.000 | equal | equal |

Every ensemble trades one axis for the other. UNION buys recall with false
positives: on the human set it reaches 0.923 recall, missing 1 of 13 errors, at
18 false positives and precision 0.400, worse precision than claude alone.
INTERSECTION buys precision with misses, dropping to 4 of 13 on the human set
and 76 of 94 on injection, below either judge on its own.

Combining two judges does not produce a grader that is better than both. It
produces a way to choose where on the recall/precision trade to sit, at twice
the inference cost, and on these two testbeds that choice was already available
by picking one judge.

### F4. The lone-FAIL specification choice, and what it does and does not move

> **Partially superseded 2026-08-24 (marked 2026-08-25).** Two numbers below are
> stale. UNION and INTERSECTION coverage is **1.000 on both testbeds** (the
> parenthetical "1.000 and 1.000; 0.983 and 0.983" was pre-repair), and the
> shared abstain budget, the `a2j` cell, is now **0 on both** rather than 0 human
> / 2 injection, because gpt-oss no longer abstains on any injection trial.
> There are also **no** lone-FAIL-plus-abstain items left on injection (was 7),
> so on that testbed the specification choice discussed below is currently
> **inert** and moves nothing at all. It still applies to the 2 such items on
> the human set. The argument itself, that the choice moves coverage and the FPR
> denominator but never strict recall, is unaffected.

Where one judge commits FAIL and the other abstains (2 items human, 7 items
injection), UNION treats the lone FAIL as a flag and INTERSECTION treats the
item as a committed PASS rather than abstaining. Neither abstains on it.

This is a specification choice, and it is the reason UNION and INTERSECTION have
identical coverage on both testbeds (1.000 and 1.000; 0.983 and 0.983). Their
entire abstain budget is the `a2j` cell, items neither judge would grade: 0 on
the human set, 2 on injection. Every other item is committed by both policies,
they just label them differently.

Its effect on the metrics is narrower than it looks. Consider AGREEMENT versus
INTERSECTION again: routing a lone FAIL on a true error to *abstain* instead of
to a committed *pass* moves that item from FN to AbsE. Strict recall is
`TP/(TP+FN+AbsE)` and charges both identically, so **strict recall does not
move** (F1). What moves is coverage, and the FPR denominator through TN.

The alternative specification, abstaining on a lone FAIL rather than passing it,
would therefore leave every recall figure in §3 unchanged and change only
coverage and FPR. It is worth stating because a reader comparing INTERSECTION's
coverage of 1.000 against AGREEMENT's 0.832 may take that as a property of
intersecting two judges, when it is a property of this one rule about lone
FAILs.

## 6. Caveats

- The human set has 13 errors. UNION's 12/13 and INTERSECTION's 4/13 differ by
  8 items; one relabeled step moves either recall by 0.077. Directional only.
- F2's injection ratio rests on 24 non-error trials. The reversal is the finding;
  0.62 is not an estimate.
- The injection suite is 118 graded trials over 9 hand-authored gold
  derivations, synthetic single-operator mutations, not naturally occurring
  model errors.
- Nothing here was selected out of sample, and none of these policies should be
  presented as a tuned or recommended operating point.
