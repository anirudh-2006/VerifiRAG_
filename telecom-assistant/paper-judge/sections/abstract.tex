\begin{abstract}
LLM judges increasingly serve as reward signals, so a judge that penalizes
surface form on semantically identical content does not merely mismeasure: it
risks teaching a preference for form. We screen for this with a paired control.
Each equivalence-preserving rewrite of a step in a mathematical derivation is
paired with a byte-identical copy of the original at the same step, so any
verdict difference inside a pair is attributable to surface form alone. The
controls are programmatic and need no human annotation. Two judges diverge
sharply. \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-5} commits six false positives across 21
covered negatives, four on rewrites and two on unmutated originals it failed
outright, while \texttt{gpt-oss-120b} commits none across 24. In two of the four
failed rewrites the judge states the algebraic equality in its own rationale
and fails the step regardless, a criterion failure rather than a detection
failure. No confidence threshold separates these verdicts from the judge's
correct detections. The same controls surfaced a defect in our own gold set. We
argue paired equivalence controls belong in any judge deployment disclosure.
\end{abstract}
