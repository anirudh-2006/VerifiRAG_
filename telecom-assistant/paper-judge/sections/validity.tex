\section{Instrument Validity}
\label{sec:validity}

What follows is a check on our own instrument, not a result about either
judge. The defect below is ours, in a fixture we wrote. Both judges were right
about it, and nothing in this section counts for or against either one.

\paragraph{A defective gold fixture.}
One of the nine hand-authored gold derivations was wrong at its first step.
The intended statement was the time-shift property of the Fourier transform,
whose subject is a shifted signal $x(t - t_0)$, but the fixture applied the
shift to the exponential kernel instead, writing the integrand as
$x \exp(-i \omega (t - t_0))$. It compounded: $x$ parsed as a bare symbol with no
dependence on the integration variable, so it factored out of the integral,
leaving a phase factor whose sign was inverted against the step that
followed. Both judges flagged the step prior to repair. The symbolic verifier
abstained on it. We re-authored the fixture and re-graded all 28 of its
trials, 18 gradable and 10 inapplicable. Every number in this paper is
post-repair.

\paragraph{Why the baseline check missed it.}
The suite screens each fixture on its unmutated steps, so that a flag on a
mutated trial is attributable to the mutation rather than to a bad baseline.
That gate dropped a fixture only when the verifier refuted a baseline step. An
abstention is not a refutation, so a fixture the verifier could not decide
passed the gate silently. The gate now separates three outcomes: refuted;
attempted but undecided; and nothing compared. It warns on the middle case
while retaining the fixture, and run against the current fixtures it flags
gold1 at both of its mutable steps, with no reported number moving either way,
since an undecidable fixture is retained rather than dropped. Warning on the third would be the same error mirrored,
since a step where nothing was compared reports nothing about the fixture. It also
appears on the human testbed as a committed pass: Q09.1 does not parse, a
Python \texttt{and} joining two SymPy relationals, so Q09.2 falls to the
no-previous-expression branch and commits as ``First step'' without ever
setting \texttt{equivalence\_checked}, a second, distinct instance of a commit
that compared nothing.

\paragraph{What the paired control did not depend on.}
The screen survived a defective baseline because the pairing does not rest on
the verifier agreeing. The two halves of a pair are byte-identical apart from
the rewrite, so a within-pair difference stays attributable to surface form
whether or not any grader commits on the underlying step, and at gold1 none
did: the verifier abstains on all 18 graded trials there. This is one defect
in nine fixtures, found during later extension work and disclosed here. We do
not claim the design was built to catch it.
