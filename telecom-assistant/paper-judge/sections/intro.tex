\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

An LLM judge is not only a measurement instrument. Preference optimization
consumes its verdicts directly, substituting a model's judgment for a human
label in training \citep{zheng2023judging}. A judge wrong in a patterned way
therefore does not merely misreport a leaderboard position; it supplies a
training signal shaped by that pattern, and if that pattern is a preference
over surface form, a model optimized against it is taught that preference.
What we look for follows: failure modes that would survive into weights.

Surface sensitivity is a recognized concern, but the usual evidence for it is a
rate compared across judges or corpora
\citep{wang2024faireval,zeng2024llmbar,thakur2025judging}. That entangles two
explanations: a higher rate can mean the judge is more sensitive to how an
answer is written, or that it was handed harder items.

We screen for this with a paired control, an equivalence-preserving rewrite
set against a byte-identical copy of the original at the same step, so a
within-pair difference is attributable to surface form alone.

\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-5} commits six false positives across 21 covered
negatives, while \texttt{gpt-oss-120b} commits none across 24. On the 21 both
committed, the discordance is one-sided, six against zero, and an exact
McNemar test gives $p = 0.031$. We
therefore report surface sensitivity as a property of one judge on this suite,
and not as a property of LLM judges as a class.
