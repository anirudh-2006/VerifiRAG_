\section{Inter-Judge Agreement}
\label{app:agreement}

The two judges were compared on the human-labeled testbed, restricted to the
181 steps on which both committed a verdict. Abstentions leave the calculation
entirely. Cohen's $\kappa$ between them is $0.306$, fair on the usual reading
\citep{artstein2008agreement}, at $87.8\%$ raw concordance.

The gap between those two figures is the correct-class majority. The judges
both call 153 of the 181 steps correct, both flag an error on only 6, and
disagree on 22, with \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-5} flagging an error that
\texttt{gpt-oss-120b} passes 20 times and the reverse twice. A concordance of
$87.8\%$ looks reassuring and is mostly the two judges agreeing that ordinary
steps are ordinary.

The practical consequence is that the two judges are not interchangeable.
Substituting one for the other changes which steps are flagged and not merely
how many: of the 13 errors in that set, both catch 4,
\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-5} alone catches 6, \texttt{gpt-oss-120b} alone catches
2, and 1 is caught by neither. A deployment that swaps judges is not holding
the measurement fixed.

Two limits on how far this carries. Agreement is not accuracy, so none of it
says which judge is right, and two judges sharing a bias would agree with each
other as readily as two accurate ones. And it does not transfer to the paired
result in \S\ref{sec:flip}: this comparison is on human-labeled steps whose
ground truth comes from a rubric, whereas the surface-sensitivity finding is
scoped to the injection suite, whose negatives are correct by construction.
