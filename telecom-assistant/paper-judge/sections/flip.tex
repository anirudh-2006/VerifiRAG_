\section{Judges Flip on Equivalence-Preserving Rewrites}
\label{sec:flip}

% Do not restate what a flip is here. It is defined once, in
% \S\ref{sec:protocol} under "What counts as a verdict flip", and a second
% statement in this section is how the two denominators drifted apart before.
% Point back to it instead.
The two judges do not differ by degree on this suite. Across the injection
negatives \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-5} commits 6 false positives on the 21 it
covers, while \texttt{gpt-oss-120b} commits none on all 24. Both judges graded
the same 24 trials under the same prompt, so the comparison is paired: on the
21 both committed, the discordance is entirely one-sided, 6 trials where claude
flags and gpt-oss does not against 0 in the other direction, and an exact
two-sided McNemar test gives $p = 0.031$.

Table~\ref{tab:flip} shows where those false positives come from. Three of
claude's ten pairs are flips under the definition in \S\ref{sec:protocol}, on
\texttt{gold3 s1}, \texttt{gold4 s1} and \texttt{gold8 s1}. Two more
are unevaluable because it abstained on the rewrite. \texttt{gpt-oss-120b}
flips on none of its ten and abstains on none.

One pair reads as a point in claude's favor and is not. \texttt{gold1 s1} is
its fourth failed rewrite and not a flip: it fails the rewrite and the
byte-identical control alike, so the pair leaves the flip count because its
halves agree, not because the objection was upheld. The two rationales do not
agree either, failing the rewrite because the leading factor needs an
additional $\exp(a t_0)$, and the control because that factor is already
correct and the bound variable should have been renamed. \S\ref{sec:validity} returns to that step.

These are not failures to detect the equivalence. In two of the four failed
rewrites the judge states the algebraic equality and fails the step anyway: on
\texttt{gold3 s1} it grants that the commuted form is equal by commutativity
and fails it for producing the factors in non-standard order, and on
\texttt{gold8 s1}, grading $3 \cdot x \cdot x$ against $3x^2$:

\begin{quote}\small
``The derivative of $x^3$ with respect to $x$ is $3x^2$, not $3 \cdot x \cdot
x$. While $3 \cdot x \cdot x$ could be interpreted as $3x^2$, \dots\ this step
appears to be re-stating the result incorrectly or redundantly.''
\end{quote}

\noindent The equality is granted and the verdict turns on presentation, a
failure of the grading criterion rather than of the mathematics.

We attach no significance claim to the flip count itself. Ten pairs over eight
step sites demonstrate the mechanism rather than estimate its rate, and the
pairs are not fully independent (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). The McNemar result
above is what carries statistical weight, and it is scoped to the injection
suite, whose negatives are labeled by construction.

\paragraph{No usable confidence threshold.}
Every verdict carries a confidence, which suggests committing only above a
threshold. It does not work here: the false positives and the true detections
are drawn from the same small set of values. On the injection suite
claude emits three distinct confidences on FAIL verdicts, 0.85, 0.95 and 1.00:
its 90 true detections are 8, 72 and 10 across them, and its 6 false positives
are 1 at 0.85 and 5 at 0.95. 80 of the 90 detections share the two values
the false positives occupy, and the first threshold that removes all 6 is
$\tau = 1.00$, which keeps 10 of the 90. There is no intermediate setting
because there is no intermediate value. \texttt{gpt-oss-120b} emits six
distinct FAIL confidences, from 0.85 to 0.99, but commits no false positives,
so a threshold has nothing to separate there either.

These thresholds are in-sample and illustrative, read off the same trials they
are then evaluated on, and they are not calibrated operating points.

\paragraph{The two judges do not agree with each other either.}
On the human-labeled testbed their agreement is fair rather than strong
\citep{artstein2008agreement}; Appendix~\ref{app:agreement} gives the
breakdown.
