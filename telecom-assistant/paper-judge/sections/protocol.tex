\section{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Gold fixtures and mutation families.}
The suite covers 9 hand-authored gold derivations of standard signals and
algebra results, independent of the 30-query retrieval benchmark behind our
other testbed. First steps are definitions and are never mutated, leaving 14
mutable step sites. Each of the 14 mutation operators is attempted at each
site, for $14 \times 14 = 196$ attempted trials, of which 78 are inapplicable
and skipped. The 118 graded trials are 94 injected errors and 24 negatives.
Three families inject a genuine error, correct verdict FAIL: \emph{algebraic},
\emph{bounds} and \emph{identifier}. Two are negative families, correct verdict
PASS: \emph{equivalent} and \emph{control}.

\paragraph{What the two negative families are for.}
\emph{Equivalent} rewrites an expression without changing its value, commuting
the operands of the first top-level $+$ or $*$, rewriting $X^2$ as
$X \cdot X$, or expanding $k(a+b)$, with equality checked in SymPy
\citep{meurer2017sympy}. The correct verdict does not move, so a FAIL there
tracks the form of an answer rather than its content, the failure mode that
propagates when verdicts are consumed as a reward signal. \emph{Control} is the
identity, the gold expression byte for byte. All 14 sites carry one, while the
rewrite arm reaches 8. A FAIL on a control is a false positive with no mutation
to blame, which makes it the floor check for the suite.

\paragraph{Paired within-item design.}
Each rewrite is joined to the control at the same
\texttt{(gold\_id, step\_index)}. Ten rewrites survived generation out of 42
attempted, spanning 8 distinct sites, giving 10 pairs, identical for both
judges. Two sites carry two rewrite operators, so two controls each serve two
pairs and the 10 are not fully independent. Within a pair everything but the
surface form of one expression is held fixed and both members are ground-truth
negatives, so a within-pair verdict difference is attributable to surface form
alone, with no rate compared across corpora and no exposure to the confound
that a higher rate may reflect harder items.

\paragraph{Graders.}
Three graders score the same 118 trials: a SymPy verifier and two judges from
independent labs, the open-weight \texttt{gpt-oss-120b}
\citep{openai2025gptoss} and the proprietary \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-5}, each
called at temperature 0. Across the 24 negatives the verifier commits no FAIL
verdict at all: 20 PASS and 4 abstains, the latter being the rewrite and the
control at each of the two mutated steps of the fixture \S\ref{sec:validity}
examines. It is a consistency check on the negative arm rather than a verdict
on every trial, and its silence on those 4 is not agreement; what its 20
commitments establish is that no negative here is one the verifier disputes.

\paragraph{Denominator conventions.}
Three conventions govern every rate we report, and none of them is the
textbook default.
\emph{Covered FPR} is computed over committed negatives only,
$\mathrm{FP}/(\mathrm{FP}+\mathrm{TN})$. A grader that abstains on a negative
leaves that denominator rather than entering it as a correct rejection, so a
covered FPR describes only the trials on which the grader was willing to
commit, and it is reported alongside coverage for that reason.
\emph{Strict recall} is $\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN}+\mathrm{AbsE})$,
where $\mathrm{AbsE}$ counts abstentions on error steps; abstaining on an error
is scored as a miss rather than excused.
A judge verdict of \texttt{error}, whether a crash, a rate-limit failure, or an
unparseable response, counts as an abstain and not as a dropped trial. It
therefore leaves a covered-FPR denominator and remains in a strict-recall
denominator.

\paragraph{What counts as a verdict flip.}
A \emph{flip} requires both halves of a pair to be committed and to differ. The
grader must commit a verdict on the equivalence-preserving rewrite, commit a
verdict on the byte-identical \texttt{no\_op} control at the same step site,
and the two
verdicts must disagree. If the grader abstains on either half, the pair is not
a flip, and it is not scored as agreement either. We report such pairs
separately as \emph{unevaluable}, with a per-grader count.
Two of \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-5}'s ten pairs are unevaluable, both of them
rewrite-side abstentions: \texttt{gold1 s2} and \texttt{gold2 s1} under
\texttt{commute\_operands}. \texttt{gpt-oss-120b} has none.
