\section{Limitations and a Proposal}
\label{sec:limitations}

The paired design is small and not fully independent: ten pairs span eight
sites, and at two of those sites one control serves two rewrite operators.
Two judges are not a survey, and what we report is a property of one model on
one suite rather than of LLM judges as a class. The derivations come from a
single domain, and the human-labeled testbed is single-annotator. The \texttt{gpt-oss-120b} injection verdicts were collected in
two sessions against the same model name, so behavior behind that name may
differ between them. The symbolic verifier abstains on 4 of the 24 negatives,
so its agreement with the negative arm is a partial check and not a
confirmation of every trial.

We propose paired equivalence controls as a required field in the workshop's
judge deployment disclosure template. A value-preserving rewrite, paired with
a byte-identical copy of the original at the same site, needs no human
annotation and can be generated programmatically wherever equivalence is
checkable. It is cheap enough to run before a judge is selected rather than
after its verdicts have been consumed.
